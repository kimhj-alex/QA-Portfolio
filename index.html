01

A warning that never explains itself

Amazon KDP · BR-006

Medium Web Discoverability
Read the full report →
Tested

Publishing an eBook with categories in both the Adult and Teen & Young Adult paths, then following the resulting warning through every layer a publisher would use: the Quality Dashboard message, its help page, and the live Amazon page.

Found

The warning says the categories conflict but not why, which one to remove, or what rule applies. The help page doesn't cover it and notes that KDP Support can't advise on categories. Declining the warning marks it "resolved" anyway, and Best Sellers Rank lists only Teen & Young Adult categories, including one I never selected.

Evidence

Nine reproduction steps and four screenshots: the warning, the categories I assigned, the help page, and the final Amazon listing.

Outcome

Rated Medium: categories decide who finds a book, and the publisher can't make an informed choice. Documented with a suggested fix: explain the rule and say which category to keep.

02

A fee that only appears at the last step

Instagram · BR-001

High Mobile · iOS Pricing
Read the full report →
Tested

Setting up a boosted post in the iOS app screen by screen, then repeating the identical ad on desktop as a baseline.

Found

On mobile, the Apple service fee reads "To be calculated" on the budget screen and is missing from the Review screen. The real total shows up only at Payment: you pay ₩106,092 to add ₩67,513 in ad funds, about 57% more. On desktop, the same ad has no extra fees, and the page says so.

Evidence

Five screenshots with the exact figures: three mobile screens, the fee explanation, and the desktop payment summary.

Outcome

Rated High: a cost increase that size, disclosed only at the final step. Desktop proved it's specific to the mobile flow, so I suggested showing the estimated fee on the budget screen and pointing users to desktop early.

03

A failing test that was the test's fault, not the app's

Shoebox Planner · Playwright

Test Automation Gen AI Evaluation
See the test suite → Related: The Ellipse Math Shortcut →
Tested

Seven shape variants dragged against every room wall in Playwright, measuring the gap between each shape and the wall.

Found

A test an AI wrote reported the rotated oval about 20px outside the room. In the app it stayed inside the whole time. The test was measuring the invisible rotated box around the oval instead of the oval itself, so the bug was in the test, not the app.

Evidence

I checked the shape by eye in the app first, then asked the AI directly whether it had skipped the ellipse math. It had. Corrected measurements: about 1.8px from the top and bottom walls and 1.2px from the left and right (Chromium). The test code is in the public repo.

Outcome

The test was rewritten to measure the true ellipse and now passes. It's the same blind spot a different AI assistant had on the same feature months earlier, so I documented it as a pattern.

Approach

"Every finding here came from actual use — not test environments, not demo apps."

My approach to QA comes from years of building, publishing, and managing digital tools as a self-employed creative. The bugs I find are the ones that interrupt real work.

  • Exploratory testing grounded in real use cases, not scripted scenarios
  • Findings documented with steps to reproduce, expected vs. actual behavior, and suggested fixes
  • Severity assessed by user impact — not just technical weight
  • Cross-platform and cross-browser testing across Chrome, Brave, Safari, and mobile
  • Accessibility and usability gaps treated as first-class issues
